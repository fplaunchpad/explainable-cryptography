Current status: **3/3 computational milestones complete under the revised
boundary**; the publication package is complete. The attack and repair results
remain unchanged. `ElectionSecurityFamily.Family.ballot_secrecy` now derives
negligible bias for the original encoded, input-bounded attacker from negligible

with the original game and its persistent-cache execution. Public input widths

completion boundary, not unconditional security of deployed Helios. TM cleanup

## Historical construction and observations

The source is Ben Smyth, *Replay attacks that violate ballot secrecy in Helios*,
May 2012 manuscript, sections 2.1–2.3, Figure 1 and section 3.1/Lemma 1
([author PDF](https://publications.bensmyth.com/files/Smyth12a-attacking-Helios.pdf)).
Use the at-most-one variant, including abstention, with at least two candidate
components. Component weeding compares every submitted component with every
component of earlier accepted ballots. Repeated components within the new ballot
are not additionally excluded. This is the manuscript's Helios 2.0 description
with the explicit Cortier–Smyth weeding addition; it is not a claim about current
deployed Helios, nor the exactly-one variant in Appendix B.

The arithmetic uses a prime-order subgroup generated by `g`, scalar field
`ZMod q`, and ElGamal `(g^r, g^m h^r)`. Honest trustee exponents and encryption
nonces are sampled uniformly from nonzero scalars. The joint key is the product
of trustee public shares. Aggregate randomness can be zero. Adversarial
ciphertexts and proofs are arbitrary inputs to verification, with no demand
that they follow honest sampling. In particular `(1,1)` is admitted by subgroup
membership, as well as by the weaker coordinate-membership test in `Z_p*`.
No nonidentity test is silently added. The attack uses only subgroup elements;
behaviour of non-subgroup encodings remains a separate model obligation.

Each component carries a disjunctive proof for plaintext 0 or 1; a further proof
certifies the product of the components. For branch `i` in `{0,1}`, verification
checks `g^s_i = A_i a^c_i`, `h^s_i = B_i (b/g^i)^c_i`, and
`c_0+c_1 = H(A_0,B_0,A_1,B_1)` in the scalar field. The vulnerable hash input
does not bind voter identity, component position or proof role. The first
algebraic results quantify over every such hash function, so do not assume a
random oracle or hash collision resistance. They cover any fixed serialization
followed by SHA-256 and scalar reduction through this interface; no byte parser
or SHA implementation is formalized by that observation.

The implemented single-trustee game reveals the election parameters, public key and
key proof, voter identities and accepted ballots with their complete proofs,
submission acceptance/rejection, the homomorphic tally ciphertexts, trustee
partial decryptions with their proofs, and the decoded tally. The attack reads
an earlier ballot before choosing its submission. The board's private state and
honest encryption/proof randomness are not handed to the attacker. The concrete
game implements these observations in `PublicPrefix` and `PublicResult`. The
completed historical symbolic theorem has its own explicitly different source
observation schedule and is preserved as it stands.

## Checked implementation boundary

`ExplainableCrypto/Helios/Computational/BallotProof.lean` implements the arithmetic
and the public disjunctive transcripts in additive notation. `0 : G` is the
multiplicative identity; scalar multiplication is group exponentiation.
`encryptWith` is the deterministic ElGamal body. `proveZero` and `proveOne`
implement real/simulated branches. `neutralProof` is Smyth's section 3.1
constructor, with no secret-key argument. `HonestSampling` samples every honest
nonce and proof coin without conditioning away collisions. `Board.submit` checks
proofs and cross-component freshness; rejection retains the existing board and
tallying continues. `Trustee` constructs Schnorr key proofs and product-group
Chaum–Pedersen decryption proofs, with exact response/verification bridges to
VCVio and checked validity of the published transcripts.

The source attack replaces a target ballot by its aggregate ciphertext in the
first component and neutral ciphertexts elsewhere. It copies the target's
aggregate proof into both the first-component and aggregate-proof positions.
Its acceptance also requires that the resulting components do not collide with
earlier board components. The checked probability statement accounts for
that event. The swapped challenge uses one abstention and one candidate vote,
so honest tally disclosure is identical in both worlds.

## Probabilistic attack and its scope

`BallotSecrecyGame.lean` defines `ballotSecrecyGame` in namespace
`ExplainableCrypto.Helios.Computational` and gives a stateful probabilistic attacker
only the public prefix for its submission and the public result for its guess.
The challenger samples a uniform hidden bit, nonzero trustee secret/proof coins,
and both honest ballots. One eligible dishonest voter submits after both honest
attempts. The public algorithm `proofReuseAdversary` copies the first accepted
ballot's aggregate and tests whether the publicly decrypted first tally is `2•g`.

`ballotSecrecyGame_proofReuse_success` proves success at least
`max(0, 1 - 6/(|F|-1))`, with a finite scalar field and injective `r ↦ r•g`.
The Lean bound uses truncated subtraction in `ENNReal`. It quantifies over every
choice of the typed hash functions. Freshness is derived inside the probability
argument; no freshness, hardness or correspondence premise is supplied by the
caller. The bound can be vacuous for small fields. It becomes greater than
one half when `|F|-1 > 12`.

This is a two-candidate, three-voter, cast-only witness with one honest trustee.
The fixed schedule enforces one submission per eligible identity; no network
authentication implementation, revoting or corrupt-trustee schedule is claimed.
The witness does not use the optional audit-and-discard ballot path. Ciphertexts
are typed subgroup elements; raw byte parsing and non-subgroup inputs remain
outside this game. Hashes and the election fingerprint are typed functions,
not a formalization of SHA or a wire format. `decodeBounded` searches from zero
to the retained board length and publishes `Option Nat`; the attacker uses the
published group tally directly. Primitive bit encodings and an efficient sampling
backend are not yet connected to a security-parameter/PPT theorem.

Independent modulo-23 controls check key and decryption transcripts, altered
proof rejection, both successful attack worlds, honest rejection with retained
board/tallying, and a fresh honest abstention with no copying effect. These
controls test arithmetic and observations, not cryptographic hardness.

## Repair and computational target

The selected concrete repair follows Bernhard, Pereira and Warinschi,
*How not to Prove Yourself: Pitfalls of the Fiat–Shamir Heuristic and Applications
to Helios*, sections 4–5 ([ePrint 2016/771](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/771.pdf),
ASIACRYPT 2012 work). `StrongBallot` hashes generator, public key and ciphertext
with the commitments. `ExpandedFreshFor` compares every component and implicit
aggregate with every component and aggregate of earlier accepted ballots.
`RepairedExecution` also strengthens the key and decryption proof hash inputs.
The disjunctive equations, homomorphic encryption, complete public result fields,
fixed eligible schedule and retained-board rejection remain explicit.

`strongHonestBallot_valid` proves actual honest proof validation for any typed
hash. `executeRepairedAttack_rejected` proves rejection for all honest coins;
`repairedAttackWorld_rejection_probability` lifts this to probability one.
`repairedHonestPair_decrypts` derives the honest group tally under distinct
component/aggregate nonces. `repairedHonestPair_rejection_le` bounds accidental
honest-pair rejection by `9/(|F|-1)` under independent nonzero sampling and
injective generator exponentiation. The probability proof derives freshness
rather than discarding collisions. Finite controls check bounded decoded tallies,
trustee transcripts, nonconstant tallying and both necessary repair components.

This instantiation retains the historical nonzero sampler; BPW writes full-field
sampling. It retains commitments in transcripts. Its cast-only real swapped-vote
game remains the scoped game above. BPW's theorem uses a different simulated-tally
game and permits corrupt trustees; neither that game nor its reduction has been
imported. The cited theorem motivates the construction and identifies proof
obligations. It does not discharge the repository's computational secrecy goal.
`repairedBallotSecrecyGame` defines the repaired public adversary interface.
The later `ElectionSecurityFamily.Family.ballot_secrecy` theorem covers the
specified encoded, input-bounded families under the explicit assumptions stated
at the beginning of this document; the cited BPW theorem supplies none of those
assumptions automatically.

The retained `Proof01.rerandomize_valid` theorem formalizes the weak-proof attack
in BPW section 3 for every commitment-only hash. Rerandomizing a ciphertext and
adjusting both responses preserves verification. A scalar nonzero shift changes
the ciphertext under generator injectivity. Separately,
`strongProofReuse_valid` shows that strong hashes alone still validate Smyth's
copied aggregate, whose ciphertext statement is unchanged. Expanded weeding
rejects it. These controls prevent either repair component being silently dropped.

generator injectivity and pointwise hash-query bounds are explicit fields.
It carries no hidden execution or PPT certificate.
`prepared_negligible_of_ddh` derives negligible prepared-game bias from
polynomial combined hash budgets, negligible reciprocal field size and
negligible advantages of the exact main and rejection DDH tests. The main
premise quantifies over every fixed accuracy degree k. Its choice
 e(n)=(n+1)^(k+1) has polynomial replay count; a single fixed polynomial slack
is not called negligible. The eventual finite field-size side condition is
derived. Standard-PPT coverage, actual sampler transport, full algorithm costs
and intended security-family policy are still obligations, so this theorem is
a conditional prototype and does not replace the computational goal.


The probability-law sampling obligation now checks in
`ElectionSecrecyFairBits.prepared_negligible_of_fair_bits`. Only private sampling
inside each exact DDH reduction is replaced by `runFairBitUniform`; the pinned
DDH challenger samples remain exact. The pinned `uniformSampleImpl` expresses
coin-only code through ProbComp's DDH interface with an unchanged output law.
For a reduction with B uniform-index queries each full DDH world changes by at
most B*2^(-slack), and the advantage changes by at most 2B*2^(-slack). Slack=n
makes this negligible for polynomial B; the actual family theorem derives B
from the existing complete main/rejection query bounds. Query ranges, modulo,
field/group operations and callbacks still need effective execution and operand
bounds. The result is conditional on security of the actual fair-bit reductions,
not a standard-PPT or physical-sampler claim.


## Loaded-word scalar remainder execution

`BinaryModuloMachine.program` now performs complete long division with finite
control and eight Boolean stacks. It reverses the raw little-endian input using
stack moves, calls the existing shift/subtract program on each pending high
bit through `TM2StackFrame`/`TM2ReturnLink`, and moves the canonical result back
in two passes. Modulo, conversion to numbers and list reversal appear only in
the theorem's interpretation, not in executed instructions. `run` assumes a
positive modulus already loaded as q.bits and the specified empty scratch;
it derives the complete canonical remainder, retained modulus, cleared scratch
and N*(8*q.size+13)+2 TM2 ticks. The invariant is r<q, giving canonical remainder
width at most q.size. This does not yet connect actual coin responses, physically
load the modulus, or hand scalar digits to the caller. Those remain C6/C7,
with physical machine/algorithm costs and intended PPT coverage still open.

### Executed chronological coin word

`CoinWordLoader` is fixed `BitOracleMachine.Code 5 4 3`: width, response,
accumulator, output and retained modulus ports. Starting from loaded width tokens
and a modulus word, it queries one coin per token and executes accumulator
reversal. `run_source` gives the whole query tree, full halted configuration and
exact charge 15*w+9 in 4*w+2 source transitions. `word_index`/`run_modulo` connect
numeric observation to the original sampler before selecting a handler. Neither
`bitsValue` nor modulo is a machine instruction in this result. Physical loading,
linking `BinaryModuloMachine` to these stacks and scalar digit handoff remain open.

### Executed coin-to-remainder sampler

`CoinModuloMachine.code` now relocates the original coin instructions and calls
an executable finite-coordinate translation of `BinaryModuloMachine` on the
same storage. The initial width is a word of tokens and q.bits is already loaded,
with q>0. `run_source` preserves the complete coin tree and derives both a common
source clock and a per-leaf charge bound. It returns canonical remainder digits,
retains q.bits and clears every other stack. `sampler_value` observes those
produced digits and equals the original modulo sampler as a query tree. The
source instrumentation charge is proof evidence, not an added protocol output.
The scalar-prefix writer/cache caller and width/modulus preparation are not yet
linked. In particular, the existing caller's free a.val.bits loading remains
an explicit C6 obligation, alongside whole physical sampler integration.
